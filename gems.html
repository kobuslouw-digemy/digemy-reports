Why Digemy

GEMS provides healthcare security to 2.4 million South African public servants and their families — a mandate delivered not from head office, but through the member services agents, claims administrators and benefit advisors who process 389,000 claims every single day. With the 2025 claims bill already exceeding contribution income and solvency below the statutory floor, the cost of a knowledge-thin frontline is no longer a service quality issue — it is a financial emergency.

Where the loss happens — an estimated R849m–R1.7bn a year:

FWA Detection Gap Claims Adjudication Errors Out-of-Network Defaults Chronic Disease Leakage Admin Re-work
“In a scheme where the claims bill has already outrun premium income, a poorly trained frontline isn’t a people problem — it’s a financial breach.”

🧠 How Digemy Optimises ROI

Digemy maps and baselines knowledge across every GEMS frontline role — Member Services Agent, Claims Administrator, Benefits Advisor and Managed Healthcare Coordinator. Spaced-reinforcement microlearning drives retention of FWA detection protocols, claims adjudication rules and network provider guidance. Real-time dashboards surface knowledge gaps by department and team, pinpointing exactly where GEMS’s R849m–R1.7bn in annual operational leakage is concentrating. Evaluation scorecards at individual, team and operational level create accountability — turning that leakage into R84.9m–R169.7m of recovered performance across the full scheme administration operation.

Top 5 Leakage Areas
Leakage 01
FWA Detection Gap
GEMS recovered R257m from fraud, waste and abuse in 2025 — down from R350m in 2024 — but global benchmarks place undetected FWA at 3–10% of medical scheme claims. With fraud analysts and claims processors relying on induction training rather than continuous knowledge reinforcement, the gap between what is detected and what slips through is compounding the scheme’s financial distress.
Loss Ratio0.5%–1.0% of claims
Annual LeakageR328m–R656m
Recovery @10%R32.8m–R65.6m
Effects
Fraudulent claims pass adjudication unchallenged, inflating the scheme’s already-stressed claims bill and accelerating solvency erosion. Systematic under-detection emboldens repeat offenders and creates compounding financial exposure across the provider network.
The Digemy Difference
FWA red-flag patterns, upcoding and unbundling detection, phantom billing scenarios and escalation protocols are embedded as spaced microlearning — tested continuously and tracked to every fraud analyst and claims processor across the operation.
Leakage 02
Claims Adjudication Errors
GEMS processes 389,000 claims daily across six benefit options — Onyx, Topaz, Emerald, Sapphire, Beryl and Tanzanite — against complex PMB boundaries and clinical protocols. Processing errors on the non-PMB pool (R22.3bn annually) generate avoidable overpayments, incorrect rejections and regulatory exposure under the Medical Schemes Act.
Loss Ratio0.4%–0.8% of non-PMB
Annual LeakageR89m–R178m
Recovery @10%R8.9m–R17.8m
Effects
Processing errors compound into financial overpayments, CMS compliance risk and member disputes that feed re-work loops — inflating non-healthcare costs and undermining the scheme’s sub-5% cost ratio target.
The Digemy Difference
Adjudication rules, benefit coding, PMB boundary protocols and Scheme rule updates across all six options are delivered as structured microlearning — reinforced continuously so the rules live in muscle memory, not in binders updated once a year.
Leakage 03
Out-of-Network Defaults
When benefit advisors and call agents lack complete network and provider directory knowledge, members default to non-preferred or out-of-network providers — generating claims 20–35% more expensive than equivalent in-network care. Poor counselling at the point of member contact is a direct, measurable cost driver on the non-PMB claims pool.
Loss Ratio0.5%–1.0% of non-PMB
Annual LeakageR112m–R223m
Recovery @10%R11.2m–R22.3m
Effects
Claims costs inflate beyond what equivalent network treatment would cost, and rising co-payments fuel member dissatisfaction and scheme attrition — compounding affordability pressure on 890,000 principal members already unhappy with contribution increases.
The Digemy Difference
Network provider protocols, preferred provider directories and care pathway guidance are embedded as daily microlearning — so every agent directs every member to the most cost-effective treatment setting, at every interaction.
Leakage 04
Chronic Disease Programme Failures
GEMS’s Chronic Disease List population represents a substantial and growing share of the 2.4 million beneficiary base. Managed Healthcare Coordinators operating without current CDL protocol knowledge fail to reinforce medication adherence and preventive care — pushing members toward avoidable, high-cost hospitalisation events that accelerate solvency erosion.
Loss Ratio0.5%–1.0% of hosp. claims
Annual LeakageR150m–R300m
Recovery @10%R15.0m–R30.0m
Effects
Avoidable hospitalisations inflate the scheme’s largest single cost driver, accelerating the reserve drawdown that has already pushed solvency below the 25% statutory minimum and driving the contribution increases that are meeting union resistance.
The Digemy Difference
CDL management protocols, medication adherence frameworks and preventive care referral guidelines are reinforced via spaced microlearning — keeping coordinators current as clinical guidelines, formularies and disease management protocols evolve.
Leakage 05
Service Re-work & Admin Waste
Poor first-call resolution, benefit misquotations and incorrectly processed claims force GEMS’s member services operation to absorb avoidable repeat contacts, internal corrections and compliance re-work. With non-healthcare costs running at R3.4bn, training-driven inefficiency is a direct multiplier on the very cost base GEMS is under pressure to reduce.

The Digemy Difference
Role-specific benefit knowledge, query handling protocols and escalation frameworks are tested continuously — reducing repeat contacts, improving first-call resolution and tightening the non-healthcare cost ratio toward the scheme’s operational targets.
