Top 5 Leakage Areas
Leakage 01
Brand Story Conversion Gap
When a R3,000 pair of jeans walks out the door unsold, it’s almost never because of the product — it’s because the advisor couldn’t justify the price. The Crown, Magriza’s table, Japanese selvedge, Zimbabwean cotton — this story IS the product, and when staff can’t tell it fluently, conversion collapses at the premium price point.
Loss Ratio1%–2% of retail
Annual LeakageR380k–R760k
Recovery @10%R38k–R76k
Effects
Browsers don’t convert. Markdown pressure builds as staff try to “just close” without story justification. The TSHEPO premium quietly erodes when the Crown narrative isn’t delivered with confidence and consistency.
The Digemy Difference
Daily brand narrative microlearning — Crown symbolism, the Atelier origin story, craftsmanship proof points — reinforced weekly via spaced repetition. Real-time dashboards flag stores where conversion is underperforming, so managers coach before the season ends.
Leakage 02
Premium Upsell Failure
A customer buying N1 jeans at R3,000 is already primed for a R900 cap, an R1,800 oversized tee, or a R300 accessories add-on. Advisors who don’t know the full range — or lack the confidence to guide a complete look — leave multi-item basket revenue on the table at every transaction.
Loss Ratio0.5%–1.5% of retail
Annual LeakageR190k–R570k
Recovery @10%R19k–R57k
Effects
Average basket value stays low. Accessories and seasonal drops underperform. Repeat-visit frequency drops when customers don’t discover full-range depth on their first visit.
The Digemy Difference
Cross-category product knowledge delivered in 1-minute daily modules, seasonally refreshed as new drops arrive. Spaced reinforcement ensures advisors know what pairs with what, not just what’s on the rack this week.
Leakage 03
Bespoke Consultation Quality
TSHEPO’s personalised jeans at R7,000+ are the highest-margin product in the range. A poor consultation — wrong fit questions, weak craftsmanship storytelling, uncertain process explanation — loses the commission and the client. One bespoke client well-served drives referrals worth multiples of the original transaction.
Loss Ratio10%–20% of bespoke
Annual LeakageR400k–R800k
Recovery @10%R40k–R80k
Effects
Lost bespoke commissions. Disappointed clients who don’t return or refer. Reputational risk in the high-net-worth segment TSHEPO is building into — where word of mouth travels fast in both directions.
The Digemy Difference
Structured consultation frameworks baked into microlearning — measurement protocol, fabric selection, styling guidance — so every Denim Consultant delivers a 5-star experience regardless of how long they’ve been in the role.
Leakage 04
Seasonal Collection Activation Lag
Each AW or SS lookbook opens a short, high-stakes revenue window. When store staff aren’t trained on new collection stories, fabrics, and styling before launch day, the critical first 4–6 weeks underperform. At a luxury brand with a small store network, a slow season start is very hard to recover.
Loss Ratio20%–30% of launch window
Annual LeakageR1.0m–R1.5m
Recovery @10%R100k–R150k
Effects
New arrivals sit unpromoted while advisors push last season’s pieces they already know. Early-adopter customers — TSHEPO’s most valuable — visit and find staff uninformed. Lookbook social momentum doesn’t convert to in-store revenue.
The Digemy Difference
Pre-launch microlearning campaigns push new collection knowledge to all 7 locations two weeks before floor arrival. Knowledge scores confirm readiness store-by-store before the season officially opens.
Leakage 05
Artisan Quality & Remake Losses
At the Victoria Yards Atelier, 12 tailors and seamstresses produce TSHEPO’s premium denim. Inconsistent knowledge of construction standards, seam specifications, and quality checkpoints leads to remakes, material waste, and delayed delivery — compressing Atelier margins and risking the premium reputation TSHEPO has spent a decade building.
Loss Ratio3%–5% of Atelier production
Annual LeakageR300k–R500k
Recovery @10%R30k–R50k
Effects
Remake costs eat into Atelier margins. Delivery delays frustrate bespoke clients. The upskilling programme intended to build the next generation of artisans becomes inconsistent when craft standards aren’t formally documented and reinforced.
The Digemy Difference
Craft standards — seam specs, quality checkpoints, material handling protocols — captured in structured microlearning that every Atelier apprentice and tailor reinforces daily. Manager scorecards track quality knowledge by individual, before errors reach the garment.
