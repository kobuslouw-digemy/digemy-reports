Why Digemy

Adcock Ingram’s OTC division — home to Corenza C, Adco-dol, Allergex and Citro-Soda — commands 22% of South Africa’s private pharmacy market by value, but that position is defended one pharmacist conversation at a time. When MSRs can’t articulate product science, seasonal campaigns miss their activation windows, and brand extensions stall in adoption, R2.4 billion of OTC revenue starts bleeding to generics and competitors.

Top 5 Leakage Areas
Leakage 01
Pharmacist Detailing & Recommendation
Adcock Ingram OTC’s pharmacy channel depends almost entirely on the pharmacist recommendation — yet MSRs who can’t articulate scheduling logic, therapeutic rationale or clinical differentiation lose that recommendation to a competitor or a generic in seconds. Corenza C and Adco-dol hold dominant category positions, but those positions are only maintained when the rep in front of the pharmacist can back the brand with science and confidence.
Loss Ratio1.5%–2.5%
Annual LeakageR36.6m–R61.0m
Recovery @10%R3.7m–R6.1m
Effects
Pharmacists route patients to cheaper generic equivalents or competitor brands when the Adcock rep can’t make a compelling clinical case. Lost recommendation translates directly into lost revenue — and over time, into lost pharmacist habit, which is far harder to reverse than a single sale.
The Digemy Difference
Digemy deploys scheduling logic, clinical differentiation and competitive counter-selling modules targeted at each OTC brand cluster, ensuring every MSR can engage pharmacists as a knowledgeable peer. Real-time dashboards flag which reps have the lowest detailing confidence scores — so territory managers can intervene before market share follows.
Leakage 02
Seasonal Campaign Execution
The winter basket — Corenza C, Alcophyllex, Adco-Napamol — drives a disproportionate share of Adcock OTC’s annual revenue, compressed into a few months of peak cold and flu demand. Adcock’s own FY2025 interim results flagged H1 weakness due to slow activation and channel stock reductions, signalling that MSR execution quality — not market demand — was the missing variable.
Loss Ratio0.75%–1.5%
Annual LeakageR18.3m–R36.6m
Recovery @10%R1.8m–R3.7m
Effects
Delayed pharmacy stock-builds mean Adcock brands are unavailable at the start of the cold and flu peak, ceding first-mover advantage to competitors who activated earlier. Each missed stocking cycle in the independent channel compresses the seasonal revenue window — revenue that cannot be recovered once the flu season passes.
The Digemy Difference
Seasonal campaign protocols, stocking threshold targets and brand activation scripts are delivered as targeted microlearning in the weeks before each winter season. Readiness dashboards confirm knowledge uptake by territory before the first promotional period breaks — so no rep enters the season under-prepared.
Leakage 03
Brand Extension Adoption Failure
Adcock OTC regularly extends high-equity brands with new formulations — Corenza Para-C, Allergex Non-Drowsy — but new SKU adoption by MSRs is often weeks behind the launch date. When reps haven’t absorbed the new product story, pharmacists don’t stock it, and the revenue window that justifies the NPD investment compresses from a full launch cycle into a slow trickle.
Adoption Shortfall25%–40%
Annual LeakageR50.0m–R80.0m
Recovery @10%R5.0m–R8.0m
Effects
Pharmacists who haven’t heard the new product story don’t stock it, so launch momentum built by the marketing team evaporates at the point of pharmacist engagement. Awareness and availability gaps in month one rarely recover within the product’s first full year on shelf.
The Digemy Difference
NPD microlearning modules deploy in days — not weeks — covering new formulations, competitive positioning and pharmacist FAQs for each brand extension. Management dashboards confirm that every MSR is quiz-ready before field activation begins, not after the launch window has already narrowed.
Leakage 04
Independent Pharmacy Range Compliance
South Africa’s independent pharmacy channel accounts for a significant share of OTC schedule 1 and 2 volume, but inconsistent MSR call quality means full-range stocking is rarely secured at independent outlets. A single missing SKU signals to the pharmacist that the rep doesn’t manage the account thoroughly — handing shelf space and recommendation probability to a competitor.
Loss Ratio0.5%–1.5%
Annual LeakageR12.2m–R36.6m
Recovery @10%R1.2m–R3.7m
Effects
Partial ranging at independent pharmacies reduces the therapeutic breadth of Adcock OTC available to patients, shrinking basket size and recommendation options. MSRs who can’t articulate range rationale lose shelf space negotiations — directly handing visibility and recommendation probability to competitors.
The Digemy Difference
Ranging protocols, call objectives and pharmacy-specific selling arguments are embedded in Digemy’s MSR microlearning path for the independent channel. Knowledge gap dashboards surface which territories have the lowest range compliance scores, so regional managers can intervene before contract renewal windows close.
Leakage 05
Generic Substitution & Competitive Conversion Loss
South Africa’s pharmacy landscape is under sustained generic pricing pressure — pharmacy assistants are increasingly incentivised to recommend lower-cost equivalents, and MSRs who can’t defend the branded premium in two confident sentences lose the conversion. Adcock OTC volumes declined by approximately 7% in FY2025 despite a 6.2% price increase, a divergence that signals demand leakage beyond pure price sensitivity.
Loss Ratio0.75%–1.5%
Annual LeakageR18.3m–R36.6m
Recovery @10%R1.8m–R3.7m
Effects
Branded Adcock products are down-traded to generics at the point of dispensing, eroding the premium margin and long-term brand loyalty that justifies ongoing brand investment. Revenue erosion compounds over time as patient habits shift toward the generic — making brand recovery progressively harder and more expensive to reclaim.
The Digemy Difference
Competitive objection-handling scripts, SEP pricing rationale and therapeutic differentiation training are delivered in bite-sized modules to every OTC field rep. Monthly scorecard evaluations measure conversion defence performance, creating accountability for protecting Adcock’s branded premium at the pharmacy counter.
