A co-funded programme that turns your hiring need into work-ready people, and gets unemployed young South Africans into real jobs.

The UIF Labour Activation Programme funds short-cycle, work-integrated learning for unemployed youth. Learners build the foundational skills, confidence and workplace exposure they need to thrive in their role, while the UIF subsidises their salary, giving you additional funding for roles you need to fill.

How it works

Three parties, one pipeline. You keep hiring the way you already do, with the stipend cost rebated.

1

The UIF funds the stipend

R2,000 per learner per month for 6 months, paid back to you as a monthly rebate.

2

You hire through payroll

Learners sign an employment contract and are paid via your payroll. You claim the rebate each month with attendance registers, payslips and proof of payment.

3

Digemy vets, onboards and trains

We screen candidates, onboard them into your business and deliver the accredited training on our microlearning platform.

The programme

Ten days of structured learning, then six months applying it on the job.

What the programme covers

The world of work

How organisations are structured and run, the responsibilities of employers and employees, employment relationships and the trends shaping work today.

Career readiness

Applying for a role, preparing for and handling an interview, and understanding an employment contract and workplace policies before signing.

Work ethics and professional conduct

Applying workplace norms and values: reliability, integrity, accountability and how to conduct yourself professionally every day.

Health and safety

Occupational Health and Safety legislation, site induction and how each person contributes to a safe, productive work environment.

Customer experience

Communicating clearly, understanding customer needs and delivering consistent, positive service in customer-facing roles.

Digital literacy and teamwork

Using workplace technology and apps with confidence, working as part of a team and contributing in workplace meetings.

The legislation behind it

How each act shapes and governs the Labour Activation Programme.

Unemployment Insurance Act

Act No. 63 of 2001

The founding legislation. It gives the UIF the mandate to finance active labour market interventions, turning it from a passive payout safety net into an active driver of job re-entry.

Unemployment Insurance Amendment Act

Act No. 10 of 2016

Expanded the UIF's mandate to fund measures that retain workers in employment and support their re-entry into the labour market, including skills development and learner placement programmes.

Unemployment Insurance Contributions Act

Act No. 4 of 2002

Creates the funding pipeline: the mandatory 2% monthly contribution (1% employer, 1% employee) collected by SARS. A portion of these pooled funds is allocated to the Labour Activation Programme.

Public Finance Management Act

Act No. 1 of 1999

Ensures strict financial oversight and accountability. Funding to training providers and private partners must follow supply chain management rules, preventing misuse of public funds and ensuring measurable outcomes.
