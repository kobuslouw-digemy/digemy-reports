Implementation, fees and the way forward

Implementation

  • No real impact on your recruitment: just ensure the requirement stipulates NQF 4 or above (not matriculants).
  • All employees qualify, regardless of level and/or actual remuneration.
  • Need to assess current employment patterns etc.

Fees

  • Per active employee per month, R 1000 excl. VAT, fixed for 24 months
  • Only on employees on payroll and active on the attendance return; part months prorated
  • No application, recruitment, mentorship or exit fee; replacements free
  • Monthly in arrears, 30 days from statement, no escalation
  • Workplace support fee passed through at the same amount

The legislation behind it

How each act and policy shapes and governs the national programme.

Skills Development Act

Act No. 97 of 1998

Establishes the SETAs and their mandate to fund workplace learning. Internships are a PIVOTAL programme, alongside learnerships, work-integrated learning, bursaries and skills programmes.

Skills Development Levies Act

Act No. 9 of 1999

The funding source. Provides for the collection of the skills development levy from employers, which funds SETA grants for internships.

SETA Grant Regulations

GN R990 of 2012

The funding rules. SETAs allocate discretionary grants in support of their sector skills plans, with at least 80% going to PIVOTAL programmes. Grants may be paid to employers, including those not required to pay the levy.

Public Finance Management Act

Act No. 1 of 1999

Governs financial oversight and accountability of SETA funds, ensuring public money is managed transparently and spent on measurable outcomes.

Employment Tax Incentive Act

Act No. 26 of 2013

The legal basis for the ETI: a PAYE set-off of up to R27,000 per qualifying employee aged 18 to 29, paid at least the minimum wage.

National Skills Development Plan 2030

National policy

The policy framework the programme aligns with, expanding access to workplace learning and linking education to employment.
