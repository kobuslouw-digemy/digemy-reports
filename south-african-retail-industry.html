Why Digemy

South Africa's formal retail sector moves over R1.36 trillion a year through hundreds of thousands of till points, shop floors and stockrooms — and every one of those moments depends on a frontline worker knowing the right thing at the right time. When they don't, the loss rarely shows up as a single line item; it shows up as till discrepancies, empty shelves, missed upsells and slow promotional rollout, repeated thousands of times a day across the country. Digemy closes those knowledge gaps at the point they occur, without adding to the training calendar retailers already run.

“Retail doesn't lose margin in the boardroom. It loses it one till, one shelf, one shift at a time.”

🧠 How Digemy Optimises ROI

Digemy layers bite-sized, spaced-repetition reinforcement onto whatever training a retailer already runs — extending its reach to every till point and shift pattern rather than replacing it. Modelled conservatively, recovering just 10% of frontline-driven leakage across the sector represents R4.1bn–R7.3bn in reclaimed margin a year.

Top 5 Leakage Areas
Leakage 01
Checkout & Transaction Accuracy
Every discount override, price check and manual correction at the till is a moment where frontline knowledge either holds or doesn't. Across a sector processing this volume of transactions, small, repeated errors compound into a meaningful annual loss.
Loss Ratio0.3%–0.6%
Annual LeakageR4.1bn–R8.2bn
Recovery @10%R0.4bn–R0.8bn
Effects
Till discrepancies erode margin quietly and rarely get flagged individually. Repeated pricing and override errors also slow queues and dent customer trust at the point of sale.
The Digemy Difference
Digemy reinforces POS procedures and pricing rules in short, spaced bursts delivered on shift, not in a classroom. Retailers see the effect in fewer manual corrections and tighter till reconciliation.
Leakage 02
On-Shelf Availability & Merchandising
Empty shelves and non-compliant planograms are rarely a stock problem — they're usually a knowledge and prioritisation problem on the floor. This is consistently one of the largest and most persistent leakage areas in retail.
Loss Ratio0.8%–1.5%
Annual LeakageR10.9bn–R20.4bn
Recovery @10%R1.1bn–R2.0bn
Effects
Lost sales from stockouts are invisible in most reporting because the customer simply doesn't buy — or buys elsewhere. Poor planogram compliance also undermines supplier and category management relationships.
The Digemy Difference
Digemy keeps replenishment priorities and planogram standards fresh in staff memory between manager walkthroughs. It extends the reach of existing merchandising training rather than replacing it.
Leakage 03
Customer Service & Product Knowledge
Frontline staff who don't know the product can't sell it, and can't reassure a customer considering a return. Both erode basket size and repeat visits in ways that rarely get traced back to training.
Loss Ratio0.5%–1.0%
Annual LeakageR6.8bn–R13.6bn
Recovery @10%R0.7bn–R1.4bn
Effects
Weak product knowledge shows up as smaller baskets, missed cross-sell opportunities and avoidable returns. Over time it also shapes how customers perceive service quality.
The Digemy Difference
Digemy embeds product and service knowledge as an ongoing habit rather than a once-off induction module, refreshed automatically as ranges change.
Leakage 04
Compliance, Cash Handling & Loss Prevention
Cash handling and returns procedures exist precisely because small deviations add up. When frontline teams drift from protocol, the retailer absorbs the cost quietly through shrinkage and write-offs.
Loss Ratio0.4%–0.8%
Annual LeakageR5.4bn–R10.9bn
Recovery @10%R0.5bn–R1.1bn
Effects
Procedural drift on cash handling and returns is a leading contributor to preventable shrinkage. It also increases exposure during audits and loss-prevention reviews.
The Digemy Difference
Digemy keeps compliance procedures top-of-mind through spaced reinforcement, closing the gap between policy and practice without adding classroom time.
Leakage 05
New Product & Promotion Launch Execution
New lines and promotional campaigns only deliver their forecast revenue if frontline staff execute the rollout correctly and consistently, store by store. Execution gaps are one of the most common reasons launches underperform.
Loss Ratio20%–30% of potential
Annual LeakageR13.6bn–R20.4bn
Recovery @10%R1.4bn–R2.0bn
Effects
Slow or inconsistent promotional rollout leaves revenue on the table that marketing and buying teams already forecast and paid for. It also creates an inconsistent customer experience across stores.
The Digemy Difference
Digemy compresses time-to-competence on new lines and promotions across every store simultaneously, so rollout execution doesn't depend on which manager is on shift.
