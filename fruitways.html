Top 5 Leakage Areas
Leakage 01
Pack-out Rate & Grading Failures
Packhouse graders working under high-throughput pressure make inconsistent specification calls — accepting fruit that will fail destination inspection or rejecting export-grade fruit to the local market. Destination-specific classification standards differ across the EU, UK, and Middle East, yet knowledge of those differences is rarely reinforced beyond initial induction.
Loss Ratio0.8%–1.5%
Annual LeakageR6.0m–R11.0m
Recovery @10%R600k–R1.1m
Effects
Preventable downgrades from Class 1 to Class 2 or local market pricing reduce revenue-per-carton by R40–R70 on affected fruit. Accumulated grading errors also erode buyer confidence and generate recurring quality deductions from established export accounts.
The Digemy Difference
Digemy delivers destination-coded defect tolerance modules to packhouse graders on a spaced schedule, reinforcing class thresholds and appearance standards across different retailer and market codes. Manager dashboards flag graders with low retention scores before they rotate onto high-value export lines.
Leakage 02
Cold Chain Management Failures
Improper pre-cooling, temperature deviations during storage, and cold chain breaks in road transport cause premature ripening and quality failures at destination — triggering importer claims and price deductions. Cold store operators and logistics coordinators frequently lack current knowledge of target temperature ranges by cultivar and destination protocol.
Loss Ratio0.5%–1.0%
Annual LeakageR4.0m–R7.5m
Recovery @10%R400k–R750k
Effects
Quality claims settled at destination cost significantly more than prevention; rejected consignments or renegotiated prices erode margin on entire pallet loads. Repeat cold chain failures damage importer relationships and put market allocations at risk.
The Digemy Difference
Digemy keeps cold store and transport operational teams current on protocol updates, with role-specific learning paths ensuring everyone from pre-cooling operators to logistics dispatchers holds the same critical knowledge. KPI dashboards enable operations managers to link training completion directly to cold chain incident rates.
Leakage 03
Phytosanitary & Export Compliance Failures
South African fresh produce exports operate under strict phytosanitary agreements with destination markets; errors in pest management records, certification paperwork, or fumigation protocols can trigger port holds, re-routing, or outright rejection at the point of entry. Documentation coordinators and compliance staff often rely on institutional memory rather than verified, current protocol knowledge.
Loss Ratio0.5%–0.8%
Annual LeakageR4.0m–R6.0m
Recovery @10%R400k–R600k
Effects
A single shipment hold costs far more in logistics, re-routing fees, and lost opportunity than the documentation gap that caused it. Repeated non-compliance with destination-market phytosanitary protocols can result in market access suspension — a catastrophic risk for an export-dependent business.
The Digemy Difference
Digemy maps phytosanitary and certification requirements to specific compliance roles, delivering targeted protocol modules that are refreshed when regulatory requirements change. Completion and assessment data gives compliance managers audit-ready evidence of due diligence at every level.
Leakage 04
Harvest Maturity Protocol Failures
Harvesting at incorrect maturity indices — driven by commercial pressure or inconsistent application of maturity testing by harvest supervisors — produces fruit that fails destination arrival tests or declines prematurely in cold storage. With multiple harvest crews working simultaneously across diverse cultivars, knowledge consistency is near-impossible without a structured reinforcement system.
Loss Ratio0.8%–1.2%
Annual LeakageR6.0m–R9.0m
Recovery @10%R600k–R900k
Effects
Immature or over-mature fruit shipped to export markets generates transit quality failures, customer complaints, and return claims that reduce grower payouts and erode buyer trust. Maturity failures are among the hardest quality failures to recover from commercially once the season has closed.
The Digemy Difference
Digemy deploys cultivar-specific maturity assessment modules to harvest supervisors and quality inspectors at season start, reinforcing starch, pressure, and brix targets through photo-based scenario quizzes. Spaced follow-up nudges keep critical thresholds top of mind throughout the full harvest window.
Leakage 05
Post-Harvest Treatment Non-Compliance
Incorrect application rates, timing, or chemical mixing of post-harvest treatments — fungicides, wax coatings, SmartFresh — creates MRL exceedances or treatment failures that compromise shelf life and trigger destination-market rejections. Packline technicians on high-volume lines frequently work from habit rather than verified current knowledge, especially during seasonal ramp-up.

Annual LeakageR4.0m–R6.0m
Recovery @10%R400k–R600k
Effects
MRL exceedances can trigger regulatory action, product withdrawals, and permanent loss of retailer codes — the hardest-to-reverse export market consequence. Treatment failures that reduce shelf life generate downstream quality claims even without a formal residue breach.
The Digemy Difference
Digemy delivers post-harvest treatment protocol modules linked to specific registered products and destination-market requirements, with role-specific assessment ensuring every technician can demonstrate application competency. Manager dashboards surface knowledge gaps before they manifest as treatment errors on the packline.
